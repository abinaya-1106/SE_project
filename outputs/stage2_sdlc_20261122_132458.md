# SDLC Analysis

## Characteristics derived from requirements
Here are the ratings and one line of evidence for each item:

1. Requirement stability: Moderate
   Evidence: "The system shall ensure that all insulin pump dosing and management activities are performed in accordance with established clinical guidelines and protocols." (FR-01)

2. Safety or business criticality: High
   Evidence: "Accurate dosing is critical for patient safety and effective diabetes management." (FR-01)

3. Regulatory and documentation burden: High
   Evidence: "The system shall provide a secure and auditable record of all insulin pump dosing and management activities, including user interactions and system events." (NFR-02)

4. System size and complexity: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall allow users to set and manage insulin pump basal rates, bolus doses, and other settings." (FR-03) and "The system shall integrate with existing electronic health records (EHRs) systems to ensure seamless data exchange and minimize duplication of effort." (FR-05)

5. Technical uncertainty: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall support multiple insulin pump devices and protocols, including basal-bolus and pump-free days." (FR-07) which implies the need for handling different technologies and protocols.

6. Need for user feedback during development: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall provide a user-friendly interface for patients to view and manage their insulin pump data, including dosing history and trends." (FR-04) which implies the need for user-centered design.

7. Can the system be delivered in parts? No
   Evidence: "The system shall provide a secure and auditable record of all insulin pump dosing and management activities, including user interactions and system events." (NFR-02) which implies the need for a comprehensive and integrated system.

---

Recommended SDLC: Incremental

## 1. Justification
The insulin pump dosing and management system has a mix of stable and volatile requirements, with a moderate level of technical uncertainty. The system will be integrated with existing electronic health records (EHRs) systems and will provide a secure and auditable record of all insulin pump dosing and management activities. The requirements for the system were derived from the reference documents by identifying the functional and non-functional requirements that are essential for ensuring patient safety and effective diabetes management. The requirements were prioritized based on their importance and impact on patient care.

## 2. Why Not the Alternatives
- Waterfall: The requirements are not entirely stable, and there is a need for continuous user feedback, which is not well-suited for the Waterfall model.
- V-Model: While the requirements are stable, the system has a high level of technical uncertainty, and the need for user feedback is not well-suited for the V-Model.
- Agile: The requirements are not entirely volatile, and there is a need for a more structured approach to ensure that the system meets the requirements, which is not well-suited for the Agile model.
- Spiral: The system is not high-risk, and the need for a large and complex system is not well-suited for the Spiral model.
- Prototyping: The requirements are not entirely unclear, and the need for a prototype is not well-suited for the Prototyping model.
- RAD: The system is not small or medium in size, and the need for fast delivery is not well-suited for the RAD model.

## 3. Risks and Mitigation
The main risks of the Incremental model for this project are:
- Incomplete requirements: The requirements may not be fully understood, which can lead to incomplete or incorrect implementation.
- Technical uncertainty: The system has a moderate level of technical uncertainty, which can lead to delays or errors in implementation.
- User feedback: The need for continuous user feedback may lead to changes in requirements or scope, which can add complexity and cost to the project.

To mitigate these risks, the following steps can be taken:
- Conduct thorough requirements gathering and analysis to ensure that all requirements are understood and documented.
- Use a phased approach to implementation, where each phase builds on the previous one and includes user feedback and testing.
- Use a modular and reusable component approach to reduce technical uncertainty and improve maintainability.
- Use a user-centered design approach to ensure that the system meets the needs and requirements of the users.
