# SDLC Analysis

## Characteristics derived from requirements
Here are the ratings and one line of evidence for each item:

1. Requirement stability: Moderate
   Evidence: "This requirement is necessary to ensure the pacemaker's ability to maintain a stable heartbeat." (FR-01)

2. Safety or business criticality: High
   Evidence: "The system shall ensure the patient's safety by preventing any errors that could cause patient death." (NFR-01)

3. Regulatory and documentation burden: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to ensure the patient's data is protected from unauthorized access or tampering." (NFR-04)

4. System size and complexity: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to communicate with other medical devices, such as electrocardiogram (ECG) machines, to provide a comprehensive view of the patient's cardiac status." (FR-07)

5. Technical uncertainty: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to detect and respond to changes in the patient's cardiac rhythm, such as atrial fibrillation or ventricular tachycardia." (FR-08)

6. Need for user feedback during development: Low
   Evidence: "This requirement specification assumes that the pacemaker firmware will be designed and implemented in accordance with the functional and non-functional requirements outlined above." (Assumption and Constraint)

7. Can the system be delivered in parts? No
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to store and retrieve the patient's medical history, including any previous pacemaker implantations or complications." (FR-05) This requirement implies a single, integrated system.

---

Recommended SDLC: Waterfall

## 1. Justification
The requirements are stable, and the system can be delivered in parts is not applicable due to the requirement FR-05 which implies a single, integrated system. The system size and complexity are medium, but the need for a comprehensive treatment plan and the importance of maintaining a stable heartbeat suggest a more structured approach. The requirements are mostly known, and the system can be delivered in parts is not applicable. The need for a secure and reliable means of communication and the importance of ensuring the patient's safety by preventing any errors that could cause patient death suggest a more structured approach.

## 2. Why Not the Alternatives
- V-Model: The requirements are stable, and the system can be delivered in parts is not applicable. The V-Model requires every requirement to map to a test, which is not necessary in this case.
- Incremental: The requirements are mostly known, and the system can be delivered in parts is not applicable. The Incremental model is not suitable due to the requirement FR-05 which implies a single, integrated system.
- Iterative: The requirements are partly known, and the need for repeated versions is not applicable. The Iterative model is not suitable due to the stability of the requirements and the need for a comprehensive treatment plan.
- Spiral: The high technical uncertainty is not applicable. The Spiral model is not suitable due to the stability of the requirements and the need for a comprehensive treatment plan.
- Prototyping: The unclear requirements are not applicable. The Prototyping model is not suitable due to the stability of the requirements and the need for a comprehensive treatment plan.
- RAD: The small or medium system is not applicable. The RAD model is not suitable due to the requirement FR-05 which implies a single, integrated system.
- Agile: The volatile requirements are not applicable. The Agile model is not suitable due to the stability of the requirements and the need for a comprehensive treatment plan.

## 3. Risks and Mitigation
The main risk of the Waterfall model is that it does not allow for flexibility in case of changing requirements. To mitigate this risk, it is essential to have a thorough risk analysis at the beginning of the project and to have a clear plan for handling changes in requirements. Additionally, it is crucial to have a robust testing process to ensure that the system meets the requirements.
