# SDLC Analysis

## Characteristics derived from requirements
Here are the ratings and one line of evidence for each item:

1. Requirement stability: Moderate
   Evidence: "This requirement ensures the system's ability to maintain a high level of performance and reliability, supporting patient care and safety." (FR-01)

2. Safety or business criticality: High
   Evidence: "This requirement ensures the system's ability to prioritize patient safety and prevent potential harm." (FR-07)

3. Regulatory and documentation burden: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall ensure the confidentiality and integrity of patient data, with encryption and secure storage protocols in place." (NFR-02)

4. System size and complexity: Large
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to communicate with other medical devices and systems, including ECG machines and hospital information systems." (FR-06)

5. Technical uncertainty: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to update firmware remotely, with a maximum update time of 30 minutes." (FR-05)

6. Need for user feedback during development: Medium
   Evidence: "The system shall provide a user-friendly interface for medical professionals to configure and monitor the pacemaker, with clear instructions and warnings." (FR-08)

7. Can the system be delivered in parts? No
   Evidence: "The system shall be able to store and retrieve patient data, including medical history, medication lists, and device settings, for at least 10 years." (FR-04)

---

Recommended SDLC: V-Model

## 1. Justification
The V-Model is chosen due to the presence of safety-critical requirements (FR-01, FR-07) and strict regulatory requirements (NFR-02). These requirements necessitate a rigorous testing and validation process to ensure the system's reliability and effectiveness. The V-Model's emphasis on testing and validation aligns with these requirements, providing a structured approach to ensure the system meets the necessary safety and regulatory standards.

## 2. Why Not the Alternatives
The following models are less suitable for this project:

* Waterfall: Although the requirements are stable, the presence of safety-critical and regulatory requirements necessitates a more structured approach to testing and validation, which the Waterfall model does not provide.
* Incremental: The system's size and complexity, as well as the need for a comprehensive testing and validation process, make the Incremental model less suitable.
* Iterative: The requirements are not entirely known, and the need for a structured testing and validation process makes the Iterative model less suitable.
* Spiral: The technical uncertainty is moderate, and the system's size and complexity do not necessitate a high-risk, high-reward approach, making the Spiral model less suitable.
* Prototyping: The requirements are not unclear, and the need for a structured testing and validation process makes the Prototyping model less suitable.
* RAD: The system's size and complexity, as well as the need for a comprehensive testing and validation process, make the RAD model less suitable.

## 3. Risks and Mitigation
The chosen V-Model may introduce additional risks, including:

* Increased testing and validation time
* Higher costs associated with testing and validation
* Potential delays in delivery

To mitigate these risks, the following measures can be taken:

* Conduct thorough risk analysis and identify potential risks early in the project
* Develop a detailed testing and validation plan to ensure all necessary tests are performed
* Establish a clear and transparent testing and validation process to ensure all stakeholders are informed
* Provide regular progress updates and status reports to stakeholders
* Consider using agile methodologies to incorporate user feedback and iterate on the system during development.
