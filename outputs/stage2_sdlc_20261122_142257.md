Recommended SDLC: Agile

## 1. Justification
The chosen SDLC model is Agile due to the volatile nature of the requirements, low regulation, and high need for continuous user feedback. Requirements FR-01, FR-03, FR-05, and FR-07 have a high priority and are expected to change frequently, making Agile a suitable choice. Additionally, the need for user feedback on the app's functionality and behavior is a key aspect of Agile development. Requirements FR-08 and NFR-01 also indicate a need for flexibility and adaptability in the development process.

## 2. Why Not the Alternatives
The following SDLC models are less suitable for this project:

* Waterfall: The requirements are not stable, and there is a high need for user feedback, making it difficult to follow a linear development process.
* V-Model: While the requirements are stable, the need for continuous user feedback and the volatile nature of the requirements make it difficult to map every requirement to a test.
* Incremental: The system is not small or medium, and the need for rapid delivery and continuous user feedback makes incremental development less suitable.
* Iterative: The requirements are not partly known, and the need for rapid delivery and continuous user feedback makes iterative development less suitable.
* Spiral: The technical uncertainty is not high, and the system is not large or complex, making spiral development less suitable.
* Prototyping: The requirements are not unclear, and the need for rapid delivery and continuous user feedback makes prototyping less suitable.
* RAD: The system is not small or medium, and the need for rapid delivery and continuous user feedback makes RAD less suitable.

## 3. Risks and Mitigation
The chosen Agile SDLC model carries the following risks:

* Risk of changing requirements: Agile development is sensitive to changes in requirements, which can lead to scope creep and increased development time.
* Risk of delays: Agile development requires frequent iterations and sprints, which can lead to delays if the team is not able to complete the tasks on time.
* Risk of quality issues: Agile development emphasizes rapid delivery, which can lead to quality issues if the team is not able to thoroughly test the system.

To mitigate these risks, the following measures can be taken:

* Regular requirement reviews and prioritization to ensure that the most critical requirements are addressed first.
* Use of agile tools and techniques, such as Kanban boards and Scrum, to manage the development process and track progress.
* Continuous integration and testing to ensure that the system is thoroughly tested and meets the requirements.
* Regular retrospectives and feedback sessions to identify areas for improvement and make necessary changes to the development process.
