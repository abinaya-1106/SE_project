Patients need to book, reschedule and cancel appointments with doctors online without calling the hospital. The booking page must show only slots that are really free for the chosen doctor, department and date, and it must stop two patients from taking the same slot at the same moment. After booking, both the patient and the doctor get a confirmation, and the patient gets reminders by SMS or email before the visit. Doctors need to set their weekly availability, block leave days and see the day's appointment list. Front-desk staff need to book on behalf of walk-in and phone patients and handle cancellations. Every booking involves personal and health information, so the system must verify who the user is, show each person only the appointments they are entitled to see, and keep a record of who viewed or changed an appointment.

Registration and login. A new patient registers with name, mobile number, email, date of birth and gender, and verifies the mobile number or email with a one-time code before the first booking. Returning patients log in with a password or a one-time code. Accounts are locked for a short time after repeated failed attempts. Patients may manage dependants such as children or elderly parents under one account, but each dependant has a separate record. Staff and doctors log in with role-based accounts created by an administrator, and a doctor must never see the appointments of another doctor unless the hospital explicitly allows it.

Searching for a doctor. Patients search by specialty, doctor name, hospital branch, language spoken and earliest available date. The result shows the doctor's qualifications, consultation fee, visit type (in person or video) and the next free slots. Search results must load quickly even when many doctors are listed, and the patient should not need to log in just to browse availability.

Slot management and double booking. Doctors or administrators define consultation hours, slot length, break times and the number of patients allowed per slot. When a patient selects a slot it is held for a few minutes while the booking is completed, and released automatically if the patient abandons the process. Two simultaneous requests for the same slot must result in exactly one confirmed booking and a clear message for the other patient. Emergency walk-ins and urgent cases need a separate path because a normal appointment slot is not the right tool for emergency care.

Appointment lifecycle. Each appointment moves through clear statuses such as requested, booked, arrived, completed, cancelled and no-show, and the history of status changes is kept. Only the right role may change a status, for example the front desk marks a patient as arrived and the doctor marks the visit as completed. A cancelled slot becomes available again immediately so that other patients can take it.

Rescheduling and cancellation. Patients can cancel or move an appointment up to a cut-off time set by the hospital, for example a few hours before the visit. After the cut-off the system tells the patient to contact the hospital. When a doctor cancels, for example because of illness, every affected patient is notified at once with the option to rebook the next available slot or get a refund where a fee was paid.

Waiting list. If no slot is suitable, a patient can ask to be waitlisted for a preferred period. When a slot opens, the system offers it to waitlisted patients in order and gives them a limited time to accept. Accepting the new slot cancels the earlier, less suitable booking.

Reminders and notifications. Patients choose how they want to be reminded (SMS, email or app notification) and in which language. Reminders contain the doctor, date, time and place but must not include diagnosis or other sensitive medical details, because messages can be seen by other people. Patients can opt out of non-essential messages without losing booking confirmations.

Video consultations. For remote visits the system gives the patient and doctor a secure link that works only for that appointment and expires after the visit. The video session must not be recorded unless both sides agree, and any recording is stored as part of the patient record under the same protection as other health data.

Doctor and staff views. A doctor sees a daily list with patient name, age, reason for visit and any notes entered at booking. Front-desk staff see all appointments for their branch, can check patients in, can see no-show counts and can print the day's schedule. Doctors can add short notes after the visit, which are stored in the patient record and visible only to authorised clinicians.

Payments. Where consultation fees are collected online the system supports common payment methods, issues a receipt and supports refunds for cancellations by the hospital. Card details are handled by a certified payment provider and are never stored in the appointment system.

Integration. The appointment system must exchange data with the hospital information system and the electronic health record, so that a booked visit appears in the doctor's record and the patient's history is available at the visit. Data exchange should use recognised healthcare standards so that other systems can be connected later without redesign. Appointment, schedule, slot and participant concepts should be kept consistent with those standards.

Reports for management. Administrators need reports on bookings per doctor and department, cancellation and no-show rates, average waiting time for the next free slot, utilisation of doctor time and patient feedback. Reports must not expose individual patient health details unless the viewer is authorised for them.

Accessibility and usability. The booking flow must work on mobile phones and on slow connections, in English and at least one local language, with large readable text and screen-reader support. A patient should be able to finish a booking in a few steps without training, and error messages must say clearly what went wrong and what to do next.

Auditing and data retention. The system records who created, viewed, changed or cancelled each appointment and when. These logs cannot be edited by normal users and are kept for the period required by hospital policy and applicable law. Appointment and patient data are kept only as long as needed for care, billing and legal obligations, and are then archived or deleted under a documented procedure.
