Healthcare software used in India is expected to handle personal and health data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and software that takes part in the national digital health ecosystem must also follow the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) guidelines. In practice this means the system must collect clear, informed consent before using personal data, use the data only for the stated purpose, let people see and correct their data and withdraw consent, protect data with reasonable security safeguards, report breaches, and share health records with other organisations only through consent-based, standards-based exchange. A hospital or clinic that wants to link patient records to the national ecosystem must use ABDM-compliant software, identify patients through the ABHA health account number, and complete testing in the ABDM sandbox before going live. Compliance and legal officers must approve how these obligations are interpreted for each project, and the rules and their detailed notifications can change, so the requirements must be reviewed regularly.

Consent and notice. Before collecting personal data the system shows a plain-language notice that says what data is collected, why, how long it is kept and how the person can exercise their rights, in English and in local languages. Consent is specific, freely given and recorded with a time stamp and the version of the notice shown. A person can withdraw consent as easily as they gave it, and the system stops further processing that depended on that consent, except where the law allows or requires continued processing. Consent records are stored and can be produced on request.

Purpose limitation and data minimisation. Only the data needed for the stated purpose is collected. For appointment booking this is typically identity, contact details and the reason for the visit, not the full medical history. Data collected for one purpose, such as booking, is not reused for another, such as marketing, without fresh consent.

Rights of the person. People can ask what personal data is held about them, request correction of inaccurate data, request erasure when the purpose is served and no legal reason requires keeping it, and nominate someone to exercise their rights if they cannot. The system offers a grievance channel with a named contact and records how each complaint was handled. Requests must be answered within the time set by policy.

Children's data. When the patient is a child, verifiable consent from a parent or guardian is obtained before processing. The system must not track or profile children or direct advertising at them, and should keep a clear link between the child's record and the guardian who gave consent.

Security safeguards and breaches. The organisation protects personal data with reasonable measures such as encryption, role-based access, logging and regular testing, and makes sure outside service providers do the same under written contracts. If a personal data breach happens, the system must help the organisation detect it, determine what was affected, and notify the Data Protection Board and the affected people as the law requires. Data is erased when it is no longer needed for its purpose, and retention periods are set per data category.

Patient identification with ABHA. Patients can create or link an Ayushman Bharat Health Account number. The system captures and links the ABHA number correctly, checks its format and validity through the official services, and avoids creating duplicate patient records for the same person. Patients can choose to use the system without ABHA where the law and the service allow it.

Roles in health data exchange. A hospital, clinic, diagnostic centre or laboratory that creates health records and shares them is a health information provider, and an organisation or app that requests records with the patient's permission is a health information user. Each role has its own technical specifications published by ABDM, and the software must implement the specification for the role it plays. Health facilities and health professionals are registered in the national facility and professional registries, and the system should store and validate those registry identifiers.

Consent-based record sharing. Health records are shared between organisations only after the patient approves a consent request through the consent manager. A consent request states who is asking, for what purpose, which types of records, the date range of the records and how long access lasts. The patient can approve, deny, view the history of consents and revoke a consent at any time, and once consent is revoked or expired no further access is allowed. The system supports both one-time transfers and periodic transfers, and every transfer is logged. Health data is exchanged in encrypted form and is not exposed to intermediaries in readable form.

Standards and interoperability. Health information is exchanged in the formats and with the coding standards that the national ecosystem specifies, using standard resources for patients, practitioners, appointments, encounters, observations and documents, so that records from different hospitals can be understood by each other. Local data models must be mapped to those standards without losing meaning.

Sandbox testing and go-live. Before production use the software is tested against the ABDM sandbox, including linking of records, consent creation and health information exchange scenarios. Test evidence is kept and shared with the approving authority. After go-live the organisation monitors the integration, keeps the software updated when specifications change and keeps working with the programme's compliance process.

Data localisation and hosting. The organisation must know where patient data is stored and processed, make sure hosting and cloud providers meet the legal and policy requirements that apply, and document cross-border transfers where they occur. Backups and disaster recovery sites follow the same rules.

Human oversight. Decisions with legal or clinical impact, such as interpreting a regulation, approving a data-sharing arrangement or releasing records in an unusual situation, must be routed to an authorised person and the decision is logged. AI-generated outputs that touch these areas are advisory until approved.
