Any healthcare system that creates, stores or sends electronic patient health information in the United States context must protect it with administrative, physical and technical safeguards so that the information stays confidential, is not altered improperly and is available when clinicians need it. For a software system this means that every feature touching patient data needs access control by role, unique user identification, audit logs of who accessed or changed records, protection of data in storage and in transit, a documented risk analysis, and a plan for incidents and breaches. Compliance is not a one-time activity: the risk analysis is repeated when the system or its environment changes, and the safeguards chosen must be documented together with the reason for choosing them. The compliance officer and the security team must approve how these rules are applied, because the legal interpretation is not decided by the development team.

Risk analysis and risk management. Before go-live and after major changes the organisation identifies where patient data is stored and moved, what could go wrong, how likely and how serious each threat is, and what safeguards reduce the risk to an acceptable level. The results are documented, a person is made responsible, and the analysis is reviewed regularly. Requirements for the software are derived from this analysis, so the requirements list must stay linked to the risk register.

Administrative safeguards. Someone must be assigned responsibility for security. Staff get access only to what their job needs, access is approved, reviewed and removed when people leave or change roles, and workforce members receive security awareness training. There must be written procedures for security incidents, for backup and disaster recovery, and for emergency operation when the normal system is down. The system must be evaluated periodically against these policies. Outside companies that handle patient data for the organisation, such as hosting or messaging providers, must be bound by a written agreement that requires them to protect the data.

Access control. Each user has a unique identifier and no shared accounts are allowed. Access is granted by role, for example patient, doctor, nurse, front desk, billing and administrator, and is limited to the minimum information necessary for the task. Sessions end automatically after a period of inactivity. There must be a controlled emergency access procedure that lets authorised clinicians reach critical information when normal access fails, and every use of it is logged and reviewed afterwards.

Authentication. The system verifies that a person is who they claim to be before showing health information. Strong passwords and multi-factor authentication are expected for staff, doctors and administrators and for any remote access. Accounts are locked or slowed down after repeated failed login attempts, and password reset must not leak information about whether an account exists.

Audit controls. The system records access to and changes of patient information, including who, what, when, from where and what action was taken. Logs cover successful and failed logins, record views, edits, deletions, exports, changes to permissions and administrator actions. Logs are protected from tampering, retained for the period defined by policy, and regularly reviewed so that suspicious activity such as staff looking at records of people they are not treating can be detected.

Integrity. Patient information must not be changed or destroyed in an unauthorised way. The system uses validation, versioning and checksums or similar controls so that unauthorised changes can be detected, and corrections to records are made as new entries that keep the history instead of silently overwriting.

Transmission security. Patient data sent over networks, including between the patient app, the web server, the hospital information system and any third party, is protected against interception and tampering. Encryption in transit is expected for all connections that carry patient data. Where a safeguard such as encryption is treated as flexible in the rules, the organisation either implements it or documents a reasonable equivalent alternative with its reasoning.

Protection of stored data. Databases, backups, file storage and laptops or portable media that hold patient data are encrypted or otherwise protected, and keys are managed separately from the data. Backups are tested regularly, stored securely and included in the same access control and audit rules. Devices and media are wiped or destroyed securely when retired.

Physical safeguards. Servers and workstations that handle patient data are in controlled facilities, access to those facilities is logged, workstations are positioned and locked so that unauthorised people cannot view or use them, and there are rules for moving or disposing of hardware and media.

Privacy and minimum necessary use. Patient data is used and shared only for treatment, payment, healthcare operations or other purposes the rules allow, and only the minimum needed for each purpose. Patients can request access to their own records, ask for corrections, and receive an account of certain disclosures. The system must support these requests within the time limits set by policy. Marketing or research use needs the proper authorisation from the patient.

Breach handling. If patient information is accessed, used or disclosed in an unauthorised way, the organisation must investigate, assess the risk, and notify affected people and the relevant authorities within the legally required time. The system must therefore keep enough logs and data-flow documentation to determine quickly what was exposed and who was affected.

De-identification and testing. Real patient data should not be used in development or test environments. Where test data is needed it is made from synthetic or properly de-identified data. Any analytics or AI component that processes patient data follows the same access, logging and minimum-necessary rules, and prompts or inputs sent to external AI services must not contain identifiable patient information.
